X-rays are one of the most commonly used forms of medical imaging. From checking a suspected broken bone after an injury to helping doctors investigate chest symptoms, X-rays can provide valuable information about structures inside the body.
Because X-ray examinations are generally quick and widely available, they are often an important first step when a doctor needs to investigate a particular symptom or condition. But what exactly can an X-ray detect, and why might your doctor recommend one?
What Is an X-Ray and How Does It Work?
An X-ray uses a small amount of ionising radiation to create images of structures inside the body. Different tissues absorb X-rays differently. Dense structures such as bones absorb more X-rays and appear lighter on the resulting image, while softer tissues allow more X-rays to pass through.
Modern digital X-ray systems capture the information using a digital detector, allowing images to be viewed and assessed by appropriately qualified medical professionals.
Medical X-rays are performed using carefully controlled radiation doses. Healthcare professionals consider the clinical need for an examination and use techniques designed to keep radiation exposure as low as reasonably achievable while obtaining useful diagnostic images.
Uses of X-rays at Crystal Radiology include investigating injuries, evaluating certain symptoms and examining specific parts of the body when an X-ray is clinically appropriate.
Top 5 Uses of X-Rays
1. Evaluating Symptoms
One of the most common reasons for an X-ray is to investigate symptoms.
Depending on the area being examined, an X-ray may help doctors assess certain causes of symptoms affecting the chest, bones, joints or other structures.
For example, a chest X-ray may be requested when a doctor is investigating respiratory symptoms or certain chest conditions. It can provide information about structures within the chest and may help guide further medical assessment.
An X-ray does not diagnose every condition, and your doctor may recommend additional imaging or testing if more information is required.
2. Diagnosing Injuries
X-rays are particularly useful after an injury or sudden trauma.
If you have fallen, been involved in an accident or injured you while playing sport, your doctor may recommend an X-ray to check for a fracture or dislocation.
X-rays can help assess:
•	Broken or fractured bones
•	Joint dislocations
•	The alignment of bones
•	Certain bone abnormalities
•	Changes following an injury
For example, an X-ray may be used to examine the wrist after a fall or the ankle following a sports injury.
Uses of X-rays at Crystal Radiology include helping investigate suspected bone and joint injuries when your referring practitioner believes X-ray imaging is appropriate.
3. Dental and Jaw Imaging
X-ray technology is also widely used in dental care. Dentists may request dental X-rays to assess teeth and surrounding structures.
Dental X-rays can help identify problems that may not be visible during a routine examination, including tooth decay and other dental abnormalities.
Different types of dental imaging are available depending on what the dentist needs to assess. For example, panoramic imaging can provide a broader view of the teeth and jaws.
The appropriate type of dental X-ray depends on the patient's symptoms, dental history and the information required by the treating practitioner.
4. Investigating Certain Cancers and Breast Changes
X-ray technology can also play a role in investigating some cancers and other abnormalities.
Mammography, for example, uses specialised low-dose X-ray imaging to examine breast tissue and is an important tool in breast cancer screening and assessment.
X-rays may also contribute to the investigation of certain other conditions, although different types of imaging may be required depending on the suspected disease and the body area involved.
It is important to remember that an X-ray alone cannot diagnose every type of cancer. Doctors may use a combination of imaging, clinical assessment, laboratory tests and, where necessary, tissue sampling.
5. Assessing Bone and Joint Changes
X-rays can provide useful information about bones and joints and may help doctors investigate conditions such as osteoarthritis.
Joint X-rays can show changes such as:
•	Changes in joint spaces
•	Bone spurs
•	Changes in bone alignment
•	Certain degenerative changes
•	Some types of injury or abnormality
Your doctor may request X-rays when you have persistent joint pain, stiffness or reduced movement.
In some situations, more specialised imaging may be required because X-rays do not provide detailed images of all soft tissues. Your doctor can determine whether an X-ray, ultrasound, CT, MRI or another examination is most appropriate.
Why Are X-Rays Still Important?
Medical imaging technology has advanced considerably, but X-rays remain an important diagnostic tool because they are relatively quick and can provide useful information about bones, the chest and other structures.
Some of the advantages of X-ray imaging include:
•	Quick examination times
•	Useful assessment of bones and joints
•	Commonly available diagnostic technology
•	Helpful for investigating many injuries
•	Can assist doctors in deciding whether further imaging is required
•	Digital images can be reviewed efficiently by qualified practitioners
